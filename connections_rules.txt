In the New York Times game [Connections](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/g/11l249l6hs&q=connections+puzzle+rules), your goal is to organize a grid of 16 words into four groups of four. Each group must share a specific common theme or connection, such as synonyms, parts of a phrase, or wordplay. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
Core Rules

* Group Size: You must select exactly four words to submit a guess.
* Mistakes: You are allowed a maximum of four incorrect attempts. If you use all four, the game ends and reveals the answers.
* One Solution: Each puzzle has only one unique solution, even if some words seem to fit into multiple categories.
* Difficulty Levels: The four categories are color-coded by how straightforward they are:
* 🟨 Yellow: Most straightforward.
   * 🟩 Green: Intermediate.
   * 🟦 Blue: More complex.
   * 🟪 Purple: Trickiest (often involving wordplay or abstract themes). [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10] 

Gameplay Features

* "One Away": If your submitted group has three correct words and one incorrect word, the game will flash a "One away!" message to let you know you are close.
* Shuffle: You can use the Shuffle button at any time to rearrange the tiles on the board, which can help you spot new associations.
* Automatic Completion: Once you correctly identify three categories, the final four words will automatically form the fourth group once you select and submit them. [3, 6, 10, 11, 12] 

Tips for Winning

* Watch for Red Herrings: The game often includes five or more words that could fit a theme to trick you into a wrong guess.
* Check Multiple Meanings: Before submitting, consider if any of your chosen words fit better in a different potential category.
* Deduce First: Try to identify all four categories in your head before making your first click to avoid burning attempts on overlapping words. [3, 5, 13, 14, 15, 16]
